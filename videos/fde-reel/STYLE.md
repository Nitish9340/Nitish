# Reference reel: editing style breakdown

Source: the 64.6 s, 720×1280 Instagram reel you sent (talking-head explainer about China's autonomous mining trucks).
How it was measured: scene detection with ffmpeg, 1 fps contact sheets, loudness and noise-floor analysis.

## Cuts and pacing
- **38 cuts in 64.6 s, about one every 1.7 s.** No shot stays on screen longer than about 3 s.
- **The presenter is on screen only about 35% of the time.** The rest is B-roll or graphics while the voice keeps going.
- **Jump cuts remove every breath and pause.** Speech runs back to back with no dead air.
- **Framing changes on almost every cut** between wide, medium and tight close-up, so even presenter-only stretches feel cut.

## Layouts, rotated constantly
1. **Full-frame presenter.** Punched in or out from shot to shot.
2. **Split screen.** B-roll or graphic on the top half, presenter on the bottom half, with the headline sitting on the seam.
3. **Full-screen B-roll or graphic.** The voice continues underneath.
4. **Floating card.** A rounded card (about 24 px radius, pale-blue border) over a blurred, darkened copy of the same shot.

## Captions
- **One word at a time.** Small (about 4% of frame height), bold white sans-serif on a tight black box.
- **Placement.** Lower-centre, or on the split-screen seam. They change on every word, so text is always moving.

## Kinetic headlines (the main motion-graphics layer)
- **Two fonts mixed.** Heavy, tight-tracked UPPERCASE grotesk (white) mixed with a small italic serif in lowercase for connecting words ("in this", "meet the", "of the rest of").
- **Stats.** Numbers sit in a **solid white box with deep-blue text** ("300", "1,200", "120%", "87%"). They slam in first, then the label builds under them.
- **Words appear as they're spoken**, line by line, so each headline builds over 0.5–1.5 s.
- **Hand-drawn white arrows** point from the headline to the subject.
- **Tech-UI overlays.** HUD lines and glowing route paths in cyan or blue.

## Transitions
- **Mostly hard cuts on the beat.** A cut lands on stressed words.
- **Motion comes from inside the frame,** not from transition effects: a punch-in, text slamming in, a card scaling up.

## Music and sound
- **A continuous music bed sits under the voice.** The track never drops below about -27 dBFS, so there is no silence anywhere. The mix is loud for Instagram at about -14.6 LUFS integrated.
- **Hits mark big moments.** The level jumps on stat reveals and headline slams, which is consistent with whoosh and impact sound effects.

## How it maps onto your clip
Your raw clip is a single static 38.5 s shot, so all of the energy has to come from the edit:

| Reference technique | In your edit |
| --- | --- |
| Jump cuts with no dead air | 14 cuts remove about 3.9 s of pauses (38.5 s → 34.7 s) |
| Shot variety | Every cut changes the framing (wide, medium, tight punch-in); longer takes get a punch in the middle |
| B-roll | Original motion-graphic scenes made for your script, so no stock footage and your content stays your own: job-title card, hiring board, work-from-home ✕, army/front-line map, onsite checklist, build vs. deploy comparison, 800% chart, comment/follow call to action |
| Layout rotation | Full-frame presenter, split screen (graphic top, you bottom), full-screen graphic, floating card |
| One-word captions | Every word captioned, white on a black box |
